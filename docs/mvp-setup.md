## Development sequence

1. Define stable lexical entries, meaning inventories, per-language explanation/example contracts, and one schedule per card.
2. Build the account-owned persistence, durable capture/job flow, per-backside one-time allowance accounting, and explicit synchronization contract.
3. Provide clearly labeled development generation/auth adapters, with realistic multi-meaning/multilingual fixtures and failures.
4. Build the principal iOS and browser study flow in the confirmed futuristic classicism style; add capture/share and configuration screens.
5. Integrate real authentication and AI providers when service configuration exists.
6. Validate same-account sync and offline conflict recovery across clients, followed by real-device share capture and distribution setup.

Use the confirmed feature scope in the product specification. Choose a coherent stack based on actual platform/library support rather than adding multiple services by default.

Keep credentials in appropriate configuration/secret storage. Developer substitutes must not be enabled as production authentication. Report real service behavior as verified only after it has actually been tested.

## Cost and allowance

The product grant is fixed: 100 successful language-backside generations once per account. Two output languages use two units; several meanings within a page do not multiply the unit count.

Measure real model usage per backside, meaning count, module selection, repair/retry, and failure. No paid-tier or overall monthly-budget design has been approved yet. Prepare operational limits and measurement; do not infer a spend budget from the 100-unit user allowance.

## Completion evidence

The MVP is complete only when the agreed platform flows work with configured services and appropriate device/browser verification. Local mocks are development aids.

Required evidence includes account sign-in/isolation, exact meaning/example pairing, quota settlement under retries/concurrency/failure, essential-language self-assessment with one schedule, synchronized content/review/deletion, actual iOS share intake, Chrome capture, browser study, and reviewed UI screenshots.
