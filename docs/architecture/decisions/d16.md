## Decision

**The Expertise modules stay with the subject, and say so plainly when a key is outside it.** The owner found the output bland and proposed a control flow in generation; the ranking found today's prompt best for keys of the subject and rejected subject angles forced onto other terms (Amdahl's law for multi-core in Mathematics: "too obscure, it feels forced"), so no control flow is added and the core prompts change in four ways. **Unrelated keys.** When the key has no meaning in the subject or a field near it (retinol, 幸福 in Computer Science), the core is one honest line saying so, in the interface language, and does not explain the key's general meaning; an established term of the subject built on the word may be named (happy numbers for 幸福 in Mathematics). **Neighbouring technical terms.** When the key is a technical term of a field near the subject (multi-core or cache in Mathematics, Frontend in Mathematics), the core says it is not a concept of the subject and explains the term briefly and plainly. **No forced angles.** No answer builds a connection to the subject that the subject does not routinely make: no formula, law or model from the subject applied to a term from another field. **Context and everyday meanings.** The core never restates or explains the Context, which only decides the key's meaning, as the [modules spec](../../specs/modules.md#input-and-applicability) already requires; and it names an everyday meaning only when a supplied Context uses it, in one opening sentence as now. Both were found in every language by the [0.10.0 review](../../history/evidence/expertise-writing-review.md). **Unchanged:** keys of the subject are explained as now; one request per module ([D11](../traceability-map.md#entries)); the writing guides ([D15](../traceability-map.md#entries)); the Languages modules.
