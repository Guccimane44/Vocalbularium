# D35: a Forms facet for English

Full entry of the [traceability map](../traceability-map.md#entries).

- **Opened:** 2026-11-13
- **Source:** the owner in a Claude Code chat, 13 November 2026, after the [English coverage check](../../history/evidence/english-wiktionary-coverage.md): option A of the [Luna and dictionary plan](../../plans/luna-and-dictionary-expansion.md#english-what-can-the-dictionary-fill), a Forms facet. Its [plan](../../plans/english-forms-facet.md) and the owner's answers of 13 and 14 November 2026: Forms shows for every word that inflects, regular ones included; it reuses the layouts facets already have, with no canvas; it is named *Forms*, tag *Fo*; the card's top line is unchanged; for a key in another language it describes the English word for the first meaning, as Etymology does; and if Luna writes forms as well as the dictionary gives them, it ships with the model alone and the English import is skipped.

## Context

An English card has seven facets, and none shows how the word changes. German has its grammar facets (Article, Conjugation, Declension, Comparison), Chinese doesn't inflect. The English Wiktionary gives the right forms for 100 of 109 common headwords and tags British and American spellings, so a facet with these forms is the one English facet a dictionary could fill ([coverage check](../../history/evidence/english-wiktionary-coverage.md)). English forms are also easy for a model, so whether the dictionary is worth it is measured, not assumed.

## Decision

- **A facet `forms`, labelled *Forms*, tag `Fo`,** the eighth English facet, after Grammar teacher. Not in the default deck; users add it from the facet window.
- **When it shows:** for a word or phrase whose English word is a noun, verb, adjective or adverb that inflects, regular ones included (*walk · walks · walked · walked · walking*). Left out for a sentence, for any other part of speech (*of*, *because*), for an adjective or adverb without comparison (*main*), and for a name or a free phrase.
- **What it describes:** the English word the card describes, by Etymology's rule: the key when it is English, otherwise the English word for its first meaning, the meaning in this Context first.
- **What it shows:**
  - a noun: its plural (*mouse · mice*); or *uncountable* (*information · uncountable*); or *plural only* (*scissors · plural only*). The note is the module's text, not the model's;
  - an adjective or adverb: its comparative and superlative (*good · better · best*);
  - a verb: third person singular, past, past participle and *-ing* form;
  - a modal, only what it has (*can · could*; *must*).
  Two accepted forms of one slot share it, joined by ` / ` (*travelled / traveled*, *learned / learnt*); never more than two. Which comes first is not a rule: the module tells no variety apart ([D34](d34.md)).
- **Layout, from blocks facets already have** (no canvas): nouns, adjectives, adverbs and modals in one line joined by ` · `, as German's Article and Comparison; a verb in the table block German's Conjugation uses, one row with a column per form: *Base*, *Third person*, *Past*, *Past participle*, *-ing form*.
- **Two core fields the card doesn't show:** `formsWord`, the English word in its dictionary form (*go* for *went*, *house* for *Häuser*), and `gramRole`, one of `noun`, `verb`, `adjective`, `adverb`, `other`. The top line is unchanged: it still shows the key's language and word or sentence, not the part of speech. Like German's grammar word, the two fields never narrow the meanings or the other facets.
- **When a stored card no longer has them** (a facet's Retry, or Forms added to a card written before), Forms is offered and the model decides from the card whether the word inflects, as for any facet whose prompt says when it applies.
- **Written by the model** until the plan's measurement says otherwise. If the dictionary's forms are better, Forms is filled from it by D32's rules and the model writes it only where the dictionary can't; if they are not, Forms stays with the model and English gets no import.

## Alternatives considered

- **A full conjugation table,** as German has: the English Wiktionary's tables are mostly archaic forms (*walkest*), and English has one form per person but the third.
- **Showing Forms only for irregular words** (*went*, *mice*): the owner chose to show it always, so a learner also sees that a word is regular.
- **The part of speech in the top line,** as German shows *Nomen*: the owner kept the top line unchanged.
- **Forms only for an English key,** as Grammar teacher: the owner chose Etymology's rule, so a German or Chinese key also shows its English word's forms.

## Consequences

- A new facet is a catalog change: a prompt, checks, about pages and samples in the six interface languages, a rubric line, prompt snapshots and the English gate.
- The English core's prompt changes for its two new fields, so every English request's snapshot changes, and the English gate runs again.
- Existing English cards and decks are unchanged; Forms appears when it is added. No migration, no API change: stored content names no facet in the contract ([D25](d25.md)), and the two fields aren't stored.
- Most cards read like *walk · walks · walked · walked · walking*. That is accepted.
