# D31: one English module, with no variety

Full entry of the [traceability map](../traceability-map.md#entries).

- **Opened:** 2026-11-12
- **Source:** the owner in a Claude Code chat, 12 November 2026, after [M3](../../history/evidence/m3-cost-per-facet.md): "we don't need British and American English in two modules. We should just have one module: English", and on varieties, "don't classify British and American English. We take what the model has generated. As long as it is proper English, we don't complain." Approved with the [plan](../../plans/one-english-module.md) ("Approve as recommended"): existing English decks and pages are renamed in place, and the change ships as its own small release.
- **Affected docs:** [modules](../../specs/modules.md) (catalog, facets, flags, the variety rule), [scope](../../product/scope.md) (modules, upgrade), [install](../../guides/install.md), `.env.example`, `migrations/0019_english_module.sql`
- **Open items:** —
- **Notion:** pending
- **Status:** accepted; implemented (0.16.5)

## Context

0.16.4 added `English (British)` and `English (American)`, two modules that differed only in their variety: a rule in `module.prompt.md`, spelling lists in `english.ts` with a check on every facet, a rubric criterion, and an accent each. In the split facets measurements, that rule made Pronunciation, written in its own request, turn an English key into the module's variety: *colour* became *color*, *The team are playing* became *The team is playing* ([M3](../../history/evidence/m3-cost-per-facet.md)). The two modules also doubled every English cost: facet folders, rubrics, calibration sets, samples, about pages in six languages, gates and voices.

## Decision

- **One module, `english` (English),** with the same seven facets, built from English (American). The two earlier modules leave the catalog.
- **No variety.** The prompts say "English" and name no variety, spelling or word of one. The core no longer says when a key is British or American. Any proper English passes: British, American or mixed on one card.
- **The key as selected stays.** Pronunciation speaks an English key exactly as selected, and an English example has no translation (`english-key-spoken`, `english-key-untranslated`). These checks are about the key, not its variety.
- **No spelling checks.** `british-spelling` and `american-spelling` go, with their lists. The rubric scores proper, natural English, and says that British and American are both right.
- **One voice.** English is spoken as `en`, in the voice named for `english.pronunciation`, otherwise `en`'s.
- **Upgrade in place.** Migration `0019` renames the module type in deck modules, components and attempts. Decks, pages, cards and audio stay as written.
- **A rule of the [modules spec](../../specs/modules.md#catalog) narrows.** Variety still comes from more parent types for languages. Varieties of one language are not told apart.

## Alternatives considered

- **Keep the key's variety on the whole card** (a *colour* card writes *favourite*), with a default variety otherwise and a check that a card doesn't mix them. The owner chose not to classify varieties at all.
- **Both words where they differ** (*apartment (British: flat)*): longer cards, and Synonyms and Examples would still need one variety.
- **A variety flag in the core, choosing the voice's accent:** a new flag for a distinction the owner doesn't want made.
- **Discard the English decks and pages,** as earlier test data was: nothing in a stored card depends on the variety, so a rename keeps everything.

## Consequences

- A card may mix spellings across facets, now that facets can be written apart. By the owner's rule this is accepted.
- Cards written before the upgrade keep their text until retried.
- The judge's English calibration set is the two earlier sets merged, without their variety cases. It has to be calibrated again before the English gate.
- A later language with varieties, such as Spanish or Portuguese, follows this decision unless the owner decides otherwise.
