# D36: Claude Haiku 5.5 is the only model

- **Opened:** 2026-11-14
- **Status:** accepted; implemented (0.16.5)
- **Notion:** [D36 in the decision log](https://app.notion.com/p/418bb13502088130a1b2f0a9036de81b)
- **Source:** the owner in a Claude Code chat, 14 November 2026, after M6 (`docs/history/evidence/m6-model-and-setup.md` on `claude/german-tables`) and the [M8 trial](../history/evidence/m8-haiku-deepseek-trial.md): "switching to haiku should be the right decision", then "no more evidence. lets use haiku as the only provider", with Haiku as the judge too. The [plan](../history/plans/haiku-only-provider.md).
- **Affected docs:** [generation architecture](../subsystems/module-catalog.md) (the stored model in its examples), [scope](../product/scope.md) (upgrade), the README's generation section, [K28](clarifications.md#k28)
- **Open items:** Haiku as judge is not calibrated for German, English and Chemistry (see Consequences).

## Context

Since 0.4 every card was written by `deepseek-v4.1-flash` through OpenCode Go, and the gate's judge was DeepSeek too. A card with every facet took 40–60 s. M6 compared it with GLM, GPT-6 Luna and Claude Haiku 5.5 on German; M7 gated Luna on every module; the M8 trial compared Haiku and DeepSeek on German, Chinese and English with Claude Opus 5.5 as an independent judge.

## Decision

- **`claude-haiku-5-5`, through OpenCode Go, writes every card,** for every module. It is the `DEFAULT_MODEL`; `OPENCODE_MODEL` can still override it for a trial. DeepSeek is no longer used anywhere.
- **Haiku is also the gate's judge.** With no `JUDGE_MODEL`, the judge uses the generator's model, as it already did. Its verdicts count once it is calibrated for Haiku on each module, by the gate's existing rule (90 % agreement, no expected fail missed).
- **Unchanged:** OpenCode Go stays the only provider and development-only ([K4](clarifications.md#k4)); one pinned model at a time (Q46, the owner's answer of 7 October 2026); no automatic fallback; the request timeout stays at three minutes.

## Why

- Under Opus, Haiku's cards were level with DeepSeek's: 14 pass, 9 weak, 1 fail against 14 pass, 8 weak, 0 fail on 24 cards each (M8).
- Haiku returned every card in M6 and M8, with no failing content checks; DeepSeek dropped 2 of 24 in M8 and 2 of 108 in M6.
- About 8 s a card against about 40 s, and about half the cost (M8).
- DeepSeek's own judge passed 20 of its 22 cards and failed 5 of Haiku's 24 on the same cards, one of them for a correct pinyin; most of the gap M6 showed was likely the judge.

## Rules set aside for this switch

- **[K28](clarifications.md#k28) and C8:** a model switch needs its own golden-set review. The owner waived it: M6 and M8 stand in for it, and no golden set or gate is run for quality before the switch.
- **A judge that scores its own model:** accepted. DeepSeek did the same until now.

## Alternatives considered

- **GPT-6 Luna:** the cheapest card, but in M7 it trailed DeepSeek on Chinese and English even after the prompt fixes.
- **Keeping DeepSeek as the judge,** or Opus: the owner chose one model for everything.
- **A full M8 first** (54 inputs a module, calibrated Opus judge, blind read): the owner decided the trial was enough.

## Consequences

- New cards and a card's Retry use Haiku. Existing cards keep the model they were written with, stored per facet ([K27](clarifications.md#k27)). No migration, no API change.
- Haiku's first calibration (14 November 2026, 171 of 177, 97 %) passed for Chinese, Computer Science, Mathematics, Joke, Modern Haiku and Traditional Haiku. It missed expected fails on German (2), English (1) and Chemistry (1): two were replies it could not score, two were faults it marked weak (*Frontend* as a chemistry term, synonyms that don't fit *break the ice*). The owner accepted this as it is: until a later calibration passes, the gate's Judge step reports those three modules as not calibrated, and their gate does not pass.
- An owner's `.env` that sets `OPENCODE_MODEL=deepseek-v4.1-flash` keeps DeepSeek until that line is removed or changed.
- Known Haiku slips from M8, left to prompt work: on a key in another language, the English Etymology described the German word (*aufstehen*) instead of the English one; in Chinese, a doubtful source for 一举两得 and 醒来 for *get up*.
