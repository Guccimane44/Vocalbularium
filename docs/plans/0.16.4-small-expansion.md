# Plan: a small expansion (0.16.4)

Draft for the owner's approval, 7 November 2026. Requested by the owner the same day in a Claude Code chat, with seven items; the owner answered every open question the same day (listed at the end).

## Outcome

Three new facets in every Languages module, two new Languages modules, one new Expertise module, and a new family with three modules:

| Module | Family | Change |
| --- | --- | --- |
| German | Languages | + Etymology, Simple translation, Grammar teacher |
| Chinese | Languages | + Etymology, Simple translation, Grammar teacher |
| **English (British)** (new) | Languages | Core as German and Chinese; Examples, Synonyms, Antonyms, Pronunciation, Etymology, Simple translation, Grammar teacher |
| **English (American)** (new) | Languages | The same facets, in American English |
| **Chemistry** (new) | Expertise | Core as Mathematics and Computer science; Equation |
| **Traditional Haiku** (new) | **Creativity** (new family) | A classical haiku in the key's language |
| **Modern Haiku** (new) | Creativity | A free-form modern haiku in the key's language |
| **Joke** (new) | Creativity | A joke in the key's language |

Per D25 nothing is shared: German, Chinese and each English module get their own Etymology, Simple translation and Grammar teacher folders, tailored to the language. The Creativity modules have no facets in 0.16.4.

**Decisions.** Items 1–4 are catalog-only (facets and modules inside existing families, no contract change). Items 5–7 need **D30**, one contract change to `ParentHeaderSchema`: Chemistry's `hasEquation` flag in the Expertise top line, and a new top-line kind for the new family. The family's colour tokens and icon go on a **canvas** before any UI code.

## Items

### 1. Etymology (German, Chinese, both English)

- **After:** a facet with a header line and a short text:

  > inherited from: Latin · Old French
  >
  > From Latin *bonus* "good", via Old French …

- **Describes:** the key when it is in the module's language; otherwise the module's word for its first meaning (`contextual` first), like German's grammar word. A French *bonne* on a German card gives the etymology of *gut*.
- **Shape:** `{ origins: string[], text: string }`. The formatter writes the header itself, the origins joined by ` · `, so multi-word names such as Old High German stay whole. The label is in the module's language and written by the module, never the model, like D29's context label: English `inherited from:`, German `stammt aus:`, Chinese `源自：`. The origin names are in the module's language too (`Latein · Altfranzösisch`, `拉丁语 · 古法语`).
- **Applies:** words and phrases only, like Synonyms.
- **Checks:** origins non-empty, no duplicate, none containing `·`, each in the module's language and script; the text within a length; no "inherited from" written by the model.
- **Prompt rule:** never invent an etymology; leave the facet out when the dictionary has none.

### 2. Simple translation (German, Chinese, both English)

- **After:** the core again in simple words: the same senses, `contextual` first when present, each in the words a beginner knows: CEFR A2 for German and English, HSK 1–3 for Chinese. A term with no word at that level is described with words that are, never left unexplained. For a sentence, one simple translation, or for a key already in the module's language, a simple explanation.
- **Shape:** one line per meaning, in the core's order, so the reader can match them; a numbered list for a word, plain text for a sentence.
- **Applies:** words and sentences.
- **Checks:** written in the module's language and script (each module's own language check); as many lines as the core's meanings plus `contextual`; no context label.

### 3. Grammar teacher (German, Chinese, both English)

- **After:** a teacher's short comment on the key's grammar, in the module's language: the points worth noticing (word order, a separable verb, a case after a preposition, a measure word, an aspect particle, an irregular form…), as a few bullet points.
- **Only when the key is in the module's language:** a deterministic `when` on the core's `language` against the module's own names for its language (German `Deutsch`; Chinese `中文`, `汉语`, `普通话`; English `English`, `British English`, `American English`), so the facet is not requested otherwise; the prompt also says to leave it out. A test covers each accepted name.
- **Applies:** words and sentences.
- **Checks:** 1–5 points, each within a length.

### 4. English (British) and English (American) (Languages)

Two modules, `english-british` and `english-american`, each with its own folder and its own seven facets (D25): fourteen facet folders. The American module is built from the British one once that passes its gate, then tailored, so the two differ where the variants do.

- **Core:** as Chinese's (no `gramRole`, since English has no grammar facets): `kind`, `language` (in English), `key`, `romanization`, `contextual`, `meanings`, with D29's label `(in this context)`.
- **Facets, in order:** Examples, Synonyms, Antonyms, Pronunciation (`spoken: { language: 'en' }`), Etymology, Simple translation, Grammar teacher.
- **What differs between them:**
  - **Spelling:** British (*colour*, *organise*, *centre*) or American (*color*, *organize*, *center*); a check flags the other variant's common spellings in the module's own text.
  - **Vocabulary:** the variant's own word first (*flat* / *apartment*, *lift* / *elevator*), in the meanings, Synonyms and Simple translation.
  - **Pronunciation:** a British or an American voice. ElevenLabs takes `en` for both, so the accent comes from the voice: `ELEVENLABS_VOICES` gains one entry per module's Pronunciation part (the speech provider already picks a voice by part); the voice IDs are chosen before delivery.
  - **Grammar teacher:** notes a point where the variants differ (*have got* / *have gotten*, collective nouns with a plural verb) in the module's own variant.
- **A key from the other variant** (*colour* on the American card) is English, so the card explains it normally and its Grammar teacher applies; the meanings may say it is the British spelling.
- **Deck:** neither is added to the default deck.
- **Folders:** `catalog/languages/english-british/` and `catalog/languages/english-american/`, each with about, names and samples in six interface languages, `rubric.md`, golden cases in `tests/golden/parents.json`, calibration cards. Names in each interface language say the variant (*Englisch (britisch)*, *英语（英式）*).

### 5. Chemistry module (Expertise)

- **Core:** the Expertise core (`relevance` subject / neighbouring / unrelated, `explanation`), its neighbouring fields physics, biology, materials science, medicine and pharmacology. Mathematics' prompt names chemistry as "unrelated", which stays right.
- **Flag:** for a key of the subject, `hasEquation` (`yes | no`): whether the concept is defined or best shown by a chemical formula or a reaction equation. Like `hasFormula`, it is kept in the top line, so `ParentHeaderSchema`'s Expertise kind gains an optional `hasEquation` (part of D30; older cards stay valid, no migration).
- **Equation facet:** only when `relevance` is subject and `hasEquation` is yes. Shape `{ latex, species: [{ symbol, name }] }`: the formula or balanced equation in mhchem notation (`\ce{2H2 + O2 -> 2H2O}`), then each substance with its name in the interface language. Abbreviation `Eq`.
- **Rendering:** the extension's KaTeX gains its mhchem extension (`katex/contrib/mhchem`, already in the installed KaTeX 0.19.0), imported in `extension/rich-blocks.tsx`; a test renders a `\ce{}` formula.
- **Checks:** the LaTeX uses `\ce{}` and renders; a reaction is balanced (atoms counted on each side, deterministic for simple formulas without charges or states, otherwise left to the judge); species list names substances only, never an arrow or coefficient. The prompt never constructs an equation the concept does not have.
- **Folder:** `catalog/expertise/chemistry/` with `sample-keys.json` in six languages, golden set `tests/golden/chemistry.json`, calibration set, `rubric.md`.

### 6. Creativity family, Traditional Haiku and Modern Haiku (D30, canvas)

- **Family:** `creativity`, label "Creativity", its folder, about texts in six languages, colour tokens light and dark meeting the contrast tests, and an icon, chosen on the canvas. The spec's planned name "Creative" changes to "Creativity" in [modules](../specs/modules.md#module-families) and [generation](../architecture/generation.md).
- **Top line:** a new header kind `{ kind: 'creativity', language, key }`, the key's language as an ISO 639-1 code, as Pronunciation's. A contract change: regenerate OpenAPI.
- **Not D15:** the family writes in the key's language, not the interface language.
- **Traditional Haiku:** three lines in the classical form: 5-7-5 (syllables in English and other syllabic languages, morae in Japanese, characters in Chinese 汉俳), a season word (kigo), a cut between two images, a concrete moment in nature linked to the key. Checks: three lines; the count deterministic for Chinese and Japanese; for other languages left to the judge.
- **Modern Haiku:** three short lines, free in count and subject, no season word required: one concrete image or moment of today's life, a turn or juxtaposition, no rhyme, no title. Checks: three lines, the whole shorter than a limit.
- **Rubrics:** form kept, in the key's language, about the key, a real image rather than a statement, natural to a native speaker.

### 7. Joke (Creativity)

- **Core:** one short joke in the key's language about or built on the key: a pun where the language allows one, otherwise a short story joke. Clean, and never about a group of people; no other limits.
- **Shape:** `{ joke: string[] }`, so a set-up and punchline sit on separate lines.
- **Rubric:** in the key's language, about the key, funny to a native speaker, its wordplay works in that language.

## Limits this touches

- **German reaches 12 facets**, exactly `MAX_FACETS`. The limit stays: a module may offer more facets than a card holds, and the user picks. Today the facet window does not stop at 12, so a thirteenth pick would be refused by the contract. The facet window greys out the remaining facets once a card or deck parent holds 12, with a short reason in the six interface languages.
- **Abbreviations** unique per module: Etymology `Et`, Simple translation `Si`, Grammar teacher `Gr`.
- **Prompt snapshots:** every German and Chinese fixture changes; both English modules, Chemistry and the Creativity modules get new ones.
- **Gate cost:** live gates for german, chinese, english-british, english-american, chemistry, traditional-haiku, modern-haiku and joke, each new module after its judge calibration.
