# Writing guide: English

## Register

Write clear, plain English, as a good English textbook or popular-science article on the subject is written for a learner. Do not open a paragraph with stock phrases such as "Imagine", "Simply put", "In simple terms", "Think of it as", "A simple picture" or "Picture …", nor with a sentence announcing that something matters. Work the use or consequence into the explanation itself: do not open a paragraph with a sentence about its importance, its use or what would happen without it, and do not start paragraphs with the same formula.

## Terms

Use the established term and say in a few words what the selection means at its first use, and do the same for any other technical term a learner may not know, as in "a hash table, which finds a value by its key" or "a prime, a whole number divisible only by 1 and itself". Do not explain everyday words. Never put one explanation inside another or between a word and the rest of its phrase; when a sentence already carries one, explain the next term in a sentence of its own. Within the length, leave out a side term rather than exceed it. Keep a common abbreviation such as CPU or HTTP and explain it once in a short clause. When the selection is in another language, say what it is called in English. Do not invent a term.

## As it is

Keep code, formulas and symbols, function names, commands, identifiers and the selection exactly as they are. Write a formula you add in its usual notation (x², n₀), not ASCII stand-ins (x^2, n0).

## Punctuation and spacing

Use British spelling, straight apostrophes and double quotation marks throughout.

## Length

at most {n} words
