# Writing guide: French

## Register

Write natural, idiomatic French, as a good French textbook or popular-science article on the subject is written for a learner, not as a translation. Do not open a paragraph with stock phrases calqued from English, such as « Imaginez… », « En termes simples », « Pour faire simple » or « Une image simple : », nor with a sentence announcing that something matters, such as « C’est important parce que » or « Elle compte parce que ». Work the use or consequence into the explanation itself: do not open a paragraph with a sentence about its importance, its use or what would happen without it, and do not start paragraphs with the same formula. French runs longer than English: stay within the length, ending a paragraph rather than adding a clause.

## Terms

At the first use of the selection's term, and of at most one or two other specialist terms a learner would look up in English sources, give the established French term followed by the English in parentheses, as in « table de hachage (hash table) » or « valeur propre (eigenvalue) »; when the selection is itself the English term, use the French term with the English in parentheses, never the English word as a French noun. Prefer the term of French textbooks to a calque, as in « situation de compétition (race condition) ». Give no English for everyday or school words, for words French specialists use only in French (navigateur, serveur), or for words that are the same or nearly the same in both languages (interpréteur, client-serveur, diagonalisation). Keep the English for terms French-speaking specialists use as they are, as in API, bug or frontend, without a French gloss. The parentheses hold the English even when the selection is in another language. Do not invent a French term.

## As it is

Keep code, formulas and symbols, function names, commands, identifiers and the selection exactly as they are.

## Punctuation and spacing

Follow French typography in prose: the quotation marks « », the typographic apostrophe ’, and a non-breaking space before ; : ! ? and inside « », but not inside formulas (0!). In prose, write decimals with a comma (3,14); keep formulas and code as they are.
