# Writing guide: Simplified Chinese

## Register

Write natural, idiomatic Simplified Chinese that reads like good Chinese popular-science or textbook writing for a learner, not like a translation. Do not open a paragraph with stock phrases calqued from English, such as “想象一下”, “简单来说”, “换句话说”, nor with a sentence announcing that something matters, such as “这很重要，因为”, “它之所以重要，是因为”, “它的重要性在于” or “它的价值在于”. Work the use or consequence into the explanation itself: do not open a paragraph with a sentence about its importance, its use or what would happen without it, and do not start paragraphs with the same formula.

## Terms

At the first use of the selection's term, and of at most one or two other specialist terms a learner would look up in English sources, write the established Chinese term followed by the English in full-width parentheses, as in 哈希表（hash table）or 特征值（eigenvalue）; when the selection is itself the English term and an established Chinese term exists, still begin with the Chinese term. Give no English for everyday or school words, such as 函数, 直线, 浏览器 or 最大值. Where no Chinese term is in common use, keep the English term, and a Chinese gloss may follow, as in API（应用程序编程接口）; never put the English before an established Chinese term. The parentheses hold the English even when the selection is in another language. Do not invent a Chinese term.

## As it is

Keep code, formulas and symbols, function names, commands, identifiers and the selection exactly as they are. Write a formula you add in its usual notation (x², n₀), not x^2.

## Punctuation and spacing

Use full-width Chinese punctuation in Chinese prose, such as ，、。：；？！（）, and the quotation marks “ ” with ‘ ’ inside them, not 「」; keep half-width punctuation inside formulas, intervals and code, as in (0, 1) or f(x) = x². Put no space around full-width punctuation, and put a space between Chinese and Latin letters or digits, as in 复杂度为 O(n).

## Length

at most about {1.6n} Chinese characters
