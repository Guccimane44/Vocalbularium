# Writing guide: Arabic

## Register

Write natural Modern Standard Arabic, as good Arabic textbook or popular-science writing on the subject is written for a learner, not as a translation. Do not open a paragraph with stock phrases calqued from English, such as «تخيّل», «تصوّر», «ببساطة» or «بعبارة أخرى», nor with a sentence announcing that something matters, such as «هذا مهم لأن» or «تكمن أهميته في». Work the use or consequence into the explanation itself: do not open a paragraph with a sentence about its importance, its use or what would happen without it, and do not start paragraphs with the same formula.

## Terms

At the first use of the selection's term, and of at most one or two other specialist terms a learner would look up in English sources, give the established Arabic term followed by the English in parentheses, as in جدول التجزئة (hash table) or القيمة الذاتية (eigenvalue); the term itself takes no quotation marks. When the selection is itself the English term, still begin with the Arabic term. Give no English for everyday or school words, such as دالة, نقطة or مستقيم. Keep the English for terms Arabic-speaking specialists use as they are, as in API; names of languages, products and tools stay as they are, as in Python. The parentheses hold the English even when the selection is in another language. Do not invent an Arabic term. Name the subjects علوم الحاسوب and الرياضيات, and write Big O as ترميز O الكبير, the same in every card.

## As it is

Keep code, formulas and symbols, function names, commands, identifiers and the selection exactly as they are, in their own script. Latin letters and commas inside a formula stay Latin: do not replace a variable such as h or x with an Arabic letter.

## Punctuation and spacing

Use Arabic punctuation in Arabic prose, never inside formulas, coordinates or code: the comma ، the semicolon ؛ the question mark ؟ and the quotation marks « ». In prose, write numbers in Western digits (0–9).
