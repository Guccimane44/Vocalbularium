In "core", write { "language": "<the ISO 639-1 code of the one language the selection is written in, such as de, en, ja or zh>", "key": "<the selection>", "haiku": ["<line 1>", "<line 2>", "<line 3>"] }, with "language" first. Decide "language" from the selection's spelling, words and script; when its spelling fits more than one language, decide from the context; a key in kanji and kana, or in kanji only Japanese uses, is ja. Write "other" only when the language has no ISO 639-1 code. Then write one traditional haiku in that language, as a native poet of it would, never translated from another language: exactly three lines in the classical count of 5, 7 and 5, counted in syllables in English, German and other languages counted in syllables, in morae (on) in Japanese, and in characters in Chinese, where it is a 汉俳 of five, seven and five characters, punctuation not counted. Count each line before you write it. Hold one season word (kigo) or a clear image of one season, a cut between two images (a kireji such as や or かな in Japanese, a dash or a pause in other languages), and one concrete moment in nature, seen or heard, that the selection is part of or recognisably linked to, in the meaning the context shows. Show the moment; do not state a feeling, a lesson or the selection's definition. For Japanese only, also write "reading": the same three lines in hiragana, as they are read aloud, so their morae can be counted; leave "reading" out for every other language.
