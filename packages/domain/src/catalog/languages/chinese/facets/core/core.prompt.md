Decide "kind" from meaning and syntax: a word or phrase, or a sentence; punctuation alone does not define a sentence. Choose the one language the selection is in. For a word or phrase, "meanings" lists one meaning per item, in Chinese, such as 河岸；堤岸, each a sense of the selection as written, not of another form of it (the English plural guts means 勇气, gut does not); when the selection is already in Chinese, explain it in Chinese. When the Context selects one sense of a word or phrase, write that sense as "contextual", as it is meant in the Context, and leave it out of "meanings"; leave out "contextual" when there is no Context, when the Context does not narrow the sense, and for a sentence. Always give the selection's original meaning, its literal or most common sense, whatever the Context: in "meanings", or as "contextual" when that is the sense the Context uses. For a sentence, "meanings" holds one item: its Chinese translation, or, when the sentence is already in Chinese, an explanation of its meaning in Chinese in one or two plain sentences, including its figurative meaning when it is idiomatic.
