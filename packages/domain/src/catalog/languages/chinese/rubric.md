- **sense**: The meanings are real senses of the selection, and its original meaning, its literal or most common sense, is always given, in "meanings" or as "contextual". Only when the Context uses a word or phrase in one of the several senses it has, "contextual" holds that sense as the Context means it, and it is not repeated in "meanings"; such a card that gives only the Context's sense, or that puts it among "meanings" without "contextual", fails. With no Context, a Context that does not narrow the sense (a word with one sense, used in it), or a sentence, there is no "contextual", and its absence is right. A selection with several readings, such as Chinese 行 (háng, xíng), keeps to the reading the Context uses: its original meaning is that reading's, and leaving out the other reading's senses is right. The card adds a label before "contextual" itself, so a meaning that writes one is a fault.
- **chinese**: Every Chinese part is correct, natural Chinese in simplified characters, the word a native speaker would choose.
- **pinyin**: Each pinyin line gives the standard reading of its characters in this word, syllable by syllable, with the right tones. Check every character with more than one reading (多音字), such as 行 (háng in 银行, xíng in 行走) or 长 (cháng, zhǎng): the wrong one fails. 不 and 一 may be written in their citation tone, as the standard orthography does, or with their tone change; neither is a fault.
- **examples**: For a word, each example uses the selection naturally in its own language; for a sentence, the one example is a new sentence of the same structure or meaning, by design, so it need not contain the selection. Each translation says the same as its sentence. When the selection is already Chinese, its examples are Chinese and have no translation, by design.
- **etymology**: The history is true to the dictionaries, never invented, and describes the selection when it is Chinese, otherwise the Chinese word of its first meaning. A native word's history explains its characters or its earlier use, such as in 古汉语; a loanword names the language it came from and what it renders. Each origin is a language or period named in Chinese, oldest first. Leaving the facet out for a word whose origin no dictionary gives is right.
- **simple-translation**: Each line says its meaning again, in the core's order, "contextual" first, in words of HSK levels 1 to 3 a beginner knows; a harder word is explained in such words, not repeated. It still means what the core says, not a different or narrower sense. A sentence has one simple translation, or for a Chinese sentence a simple explanation.
- **grammar-teacher**: Only for a Chinese selection. Each point is a true and useful note on the selection's own grammar, such as its measure word, an aspect particle, word order, 把 or 被, or a complement, as a teacher would give it, in correct Chinese; an example, when given, is natural and uses the selection. A point on grammar the selection does not have, or that is wrong, fails.
