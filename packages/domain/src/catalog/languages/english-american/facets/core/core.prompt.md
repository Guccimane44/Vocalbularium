In "core", write { "kind": "<word | sentence>", "language": "<the English name of the selection's language, such as Chinese, or English when it is English>", "key": "<the selection>", "romanization": "<its romanization, only when useful>", "contextual": "<for a word only, when the Context selects one of its senses: that sense, in American English>", "meanings": ["<...>"] }, with "kind" first. Decide "kind" from meaning and syntax: a word or phrase, or a sentence; punctuation alone does not define a sentence. Choose the one language the selection is in; a selection in British English, such as colour or flat, is English. For a word or phrase, "meanings" lists one meaning per item, in American English, such as the side of a river, each a sense of the selection as written, not of another form of it (the French plural lunettes means glasses, lunette does not); when the selection is already in English, explain it in English, as an English dictionary does, and say when it is the British spelling or the British word, such as the British spelling of color. Write "contextual" only when the input has a "context" that uses the selection in one of the several senses it has: that sense, in American English, as the context means it, left out of "meanings". Otherwise leave "contextual" out: with no "context", with a context that does not narrow the sense, for a word with one sense, and for a sentence. Every meaning is a sense the selection itself has in its own language, never a sense of an English word that looks or sounds like it. Always give the selection's original meaning, its literal or most common sense, whatever the context: in "meanings", or as "contextual" when that is the sense the context uses. A selection with several readings, such as Chinese 行 (háng, xíng), keeps to the reading the context uses: its original meaning is that reading's, and senses of its other readings are left out. For breadcrumb in a context about a user interface, "contextual" is a trail of links showing where a page sits in a website, and "meanings" holds a small crumb of bread. Never write a label such as "in this context": the card adds it. Write no field the shape above does not name. For a sentence, "meanings" holds one item: its American English translation, or, when the sentence is already in English, an explanation of its meaning in English in one or two plain sentences, including its figurative meaning when it is idiomatic. Leave out "romanization" when it is not useful.
