- **sense**: The meanings are real senses of the selection, and its original meaning, its literal or most common sense, is always given, in "meanings" or as "contextual". Only when the Context uses a word or phrase in one of the several senses it has, "contextual" holds that sense as the Context means it, and it is not repeated in "meanings"; such a card that gives only the Context's sense, or that puts it among "meanings" without "contextual", fails. With no Context, a Context that does not narrow the sense (a word with one sense, used in it), or a sentence, there is no "contextual", and its absence is right. A selection with several readings, such as Chinese 行 (háng, xíng), keeps to the reading the Context uses: its original meaning is that reading's, and leaving out the other reading's senses is right. The card adds a label before "contextual" itself, so a meaning that writes one is a fault. An English selection is explained in English, as an English dictionary does, by design; a British spelling or word as the selection (colour, flat) is English, and saying it is British is right.
- **american**: Every English part the card writes is correct, natural American English a learner can trust: American spelling (color, center, organize, traveled, gray) and the American word where the variants differ (apartment, elevator, fall, sidewalk). A British spelling or word in the card's own English, other than the selection itself, is a fault; in an example sentence that is weak unless it is the card's own American English.
- **facets**: Every facet present is right for this word and gives what its name promises. Synonyms that fit at least one of the meanings (a near-synonym is at worst weak); antonyms that are true opposites of a meaning (a word that is merely related or contrasting fails). The etymology is the one a dictionary gives for the English word the card describes (the selection when it is English, otherwise the English word for its first meaning, by design), its origins oldest first; an invented or wrong etymology fails. The simple translation says the same as each meaning, in its order, in words a learner at CEFR A2 knows. Grammar teacher appears only for an English selection, and its points are true of it and worth a learner's notice; a wrong rule fails.
- **examples**: For a word, each example uses the selection naturally in its own language; for a sentence, the one example is a new sentence of the same structure or meaning, by design, so it need not contain the selection. Each translation says the same as its sentence. When the selection is already English, its examples are English and have no translation, by design.
