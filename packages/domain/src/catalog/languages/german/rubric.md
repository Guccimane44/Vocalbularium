- **sense**: The meanings are real senses of the selection, and its original meaning, its literal or most common sense, is always given, in "meanings" or as "contextual". Only when the Context uses a word or phrase in one of the several senses it has, "contextual" holds that sense as the Context means it, and it is not repeated in "meanings"; such a card that gives only the Context's sense, or that puts it among "meanings" without "contextual", fails. With no Context, a Context that does not narrow the sense (a word with one sense, used in it), or a sentence, there is no "contextual", and its absence is right. A selection with several readings, such as Chinese 行 (háng, xíng), keeps to the reading the Context uses: its original meaning is that reading's, and leaving out the other reading's senses is right. The card adds a label before "contextual" itself, so a meaning that writes one is a fault.
- **german**: Every German part is correct, natural German a learner can trust: spelling, grammar, an article's gender and a plural's form.
- **facets**: Every facet present is right for this word and gives what its name promises. The grammar facets (article, conjugation, declension, comparison, case) describe the German translation's grammar word, not the selection itself, by design: a Chinese idiom translated by a German verb has that verb's conjugation. Check them: synonyms and antonyms that fit at least one of the meanings (a near-synonym is at worst weak), antonyms that are true opposites of a meaning (a word that is merely related or contrasting fails), the right article, correct conjugation or declension forms.
- **examples**: For a word, each example uses the selection naturally in its own language; for a sentence, the one example is a new sentence of the same structure or meaning, by design, so it need not contain the selection. Each translation says the same as its sentence. When the selection is already German, its examples are German and have no translation, by design.
- **etymology**: For a word, Etymology is the grammar word's real etymology as a dictionary gives it: the key when it is German, otherwise the German word for its first meaning, "contextual" first when the core has one (so for breadcrumb read as Navigationspfad it is the etymology of Pfad or Navigation, not of Brotkrümel), by design. Its origins are the languages it came from or through, named in German; its text says its earlier forms and how it reached today's meaning. An invented or guessed etymology, or one of another word, fails; a word whose etymology is unknown has no Etymology, and its absence is right. The card writes "stammt aus:" before the origins itself, so origins without it are right and never weak.
- **simple-translation**: Simple translation says each of the core's meanings again, in the same order, "contextual" first, in German a learner at CEFR A2 understands; for a sentence, one simple German line. A line that says something other than its meaning fails; a line that uses words well above A2 where simpler ones exist, or leaves part of its meaning out, is weak; a hard term is explained in simple words rather than left out.
- **grammar-teacher**: Grammar teacher is present only for a German key, by design, and its absence for any other key is right. Each point is true of the key and worth a learner's notice (a separable verb, a case after a preposition, an irregular form, word order); a wrong point fails, a trivial one is weak.
