Decide "kind" from meaning and syntax: a word or phrase, or a sentence; punctuation alone does not define a sentence. Choose the one language the selection is in. For a word or phrase, "meanings" lists one meaning per item, in German, such as Ufer eines Flusses; when the selection is already in German, explain it in German. Write "contextual" only when the input has a "context" that uses the selection in one of the several senses it has: that sense, in German, as the context means it, left out of "meanings". Otherwise leave "contextual" out: with no "context", with a context that does not narrow the sense, for a word with one sense, and for a sentence. Every meaning is a sense the selection itself has in its own language, never a sense of a German word that looks or sounds like it. Always give the selection's original meaning, its literal or most common sense, whatever the context: in "meanings", or as "contextual" when that is the sense the context uses. A selection with several readings, such as Chinese 行 (háng, xíng), keeps to the reading the context uses: its original meaning is that reading's, and senses of its other readings are left out. For breadcrumb in a context about a user interface, "contextual" is Navigationspfad in einer Benutzeroberfläche, and "meanings" holds Brotkrümel. Never write a label such as "in diesem Zusammenhang": the card adds it. Write no field the shape above does not name. For a sentence, "meanings" holds one item: its German translation, or, when the sentence is already in German, an explanation of its meaning in German in one or two plain sentences, including its figurative meaning when it is idiomatic; leave out "gramRole". Leave out "romanization" when it is not useful.
